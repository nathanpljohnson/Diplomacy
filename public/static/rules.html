THE FIELD RULEBOOK · CLASSIC EUROPE, 1901

The art of keeping
your word. Or not.

Seven powers negotiate over a shared map. Everyone writes orders in secret. Those orders resolve simultaneously, regardless of the promises that preceded them. Standard games use no dice; the optional drunk-country house rule uses random order selection.

This is an original practical reference for the rules implemented by Entente. The Diplomacy engine resolves orders, including support cuts, convoy disruption, retreats, and adjustments. Its documented rule interpretations govern edge cases.

01. The board and the objective

Classic Europe has 75 playable provinces: 56 land provinces and 19 seas. Switzerland is impassable. Of the 34 supply centers, 22 are home centers and 12 begin neutral. Austria-Hungary, England, France, Germany, Italy, and Turkey start with three units each. Russia starts with four, including a fleet on St Petersburg’s south coast.

A power wins by owning 18 supply centers. Ownership of a supply center changes when a unit occupies it at the end of the fall turn, after any retreats. Passing through a center in spring does not capture it. An owned center stays yours when you leave until another power captures it in fall.

02. Movement and holding

Armies occupy land provinces. Fleets occupy seas or coastal provinces. An army can move into an adjacent land province; a fleet can move to an adjacent sea or along connected coastlines. A province can hold only one unit. All units have the same basic strength of one.

A PAR H holds the army in Paris. A PAR - BUR orders it to Burgundy. A missing movement order is treated as a hold. An invalid order does not become a successful move; Entente’s builder filters for legal choices before submission.

Equal-strength attacks on the same province bounce. A move against an equal-strength holding unit also fails. A moving unit that fails normally stays where it started. A unit that successfully leaves can make room for another unit, including in a circular chain of three or more moves.

Direct exchanges between opposing units normally bounce unless one has enough strength to dislodge the other; a move by convoy is an exception to ordinary head-to-head treatment. You cannot dislodge your own unit or use your own support to make that dislodgement happen.

Special borders and coasts

Spain has north and south coasts, Bulgaria has east and south coasts, and St Petersburg has north and south coasts. Fleet orders must choose a reachable coast, such as F STP/SC - BOT. A fleet cannot switch between two coasts of a province just by remaining there. An army occupies the province without a coast designation.

Kiel and Constantinople allow fleets to pass between the seas connected through those provinces. Denmark and Sweden are adjacent to both armies and fleets. Switzerland cannot be entered. The order builder uses the standard map’s unit-specific adjacency and coast data, rather than inferring legal moves from the drawing.

03. Support and dislodgement

A unit may support another unit holding or moving into a province that the supporting unit could itself enter. It does not move when supporting. A MAR S A PAR - BUR adds one strength to the army moving from Paris to Burgundy. A GAS S A PAR supports the army in Paris holding.

Support must match the other unit’s actual order. Supporting a hold does not strengthen that unit’s attempted move. A move with one valid support has strength two; additional valid supports add strength. A stronger attack dislodges a defender that does not successfully leave.

An attack on a supporting unit normally cuts its support even if that attack fails. An attack coming from the province against which support is being given does not cut that support merely by attacking; dislodging the supporter does invalidate it. Dislodged units have additional effects handled by the engine, including the limits on cutting support and influencing the province they were attacked from.

Support can assist any power. It is not an enforceable alliance, and a support order is only useful if the supported action occurs.

04. Moving an army by sea

Fleets in sea provinces can convoy an army between coastal land provinces. The fleet or chain of fleets must connect the departure coast to the destination coast. A fleet in a coastal land province cannot form part of the convoy.

The army must order its move via convoy, for example A LON - BEL VIA. The fleet must order the matching convoy, for example F NTH C A LON - BEL. Every fleet in a longer route needs the matching convoy order. The army attacks the destination with its normal strength, which can be supported.

A convoying fleet being attacked does not itself break the convoy. Dislodgement can break the route. If at least one complete authorized route survives, a convoy may still succeed. Convoy paradoxes use the adjudicator’s tested interpretation; they are not resolved by the interface or by a rulebook text generator.

05. Retreats

A dislodged unit must retreat to an eligible adjacent province or disband. It cannot retreat to an occupied province, the province the dislodging attack came from, or a province where a standoff occurred in the preceding movement phase. A retreat cannot use a convoy and cannot be supported.

A PAR R PIC retreats the dislodged army from Paris to Picardy. If two dislodged units retreat to the same province, both are disbanded. A missing or invalid retreat causes disbandment. Entente shows the engine’s legal retreat choices beside the map.

06. Winter adjustments

Rules edition note. This engine uses the legacy automatic-removal rule: missing disbands are selected by distance from home supply centers. The 2023 rules measure distance from owned supply centers instead. Enter explicit removal orders if you want to avoid relying on that fallback. The current release is not certified against every case in the newer DATC 3.3 suite.

After fall, compare each power’s unit count with its supply center count. A power with more centers than units may build the difference. It may build only in an unoccupied home supply center that it still owns. A fleet requires a coastal home center and, where applicable, an explicit coast.

A PAR B builds an army in Paris. F BRE B builds a fleet in Brest. A power may waive any or all builds. A power with more units than centers must remove the difference, using orders such as A BUR D.

Missing build orders are waived. Missing required removals use the engine’s civil disorder policy: units farthest from home are removed first, with its documented tie-breaking rules. Entente validates the number of entered adjustments and permits only the engine’s legal build or disband options.

07. Playing at this table

Create a game as a host only, or claim a power yourself. Share the invitation code privately; each signed-in friend claims one available power. Current orders remain private until resolution.

Save draft stores a sheet without marking it ready. Submit orders stores it and marks it ready. Both saved sheets are used at resolution; edits left only in your browser are not. You can replace your sheet until the phase ends. No saved orders means the normal missing-order defaults apply: holds in movement, disbands for missing retreats, waived builds and automatic removals where necessary.

The host can resolve manually or start a phase clock, adjust durations, pause, and resume it. Suggested durations are 20 minutes for 1901 movement phases, 15 for later movements, 3 for retreats, and 5 for adjustments. Each next phase receives its configured duration. Signed scheduled callbacks resolve deadlines even when everyone closes the site. A scheduler failure pauses the clock and displays an error for the host.

Draw instead of moves — this table’s custom rule

Choose either a move sheet or a draw offer for the phase. Submitting a draw offer replaces your orders. If every surviving power offers a draw, resolution ends the game and prevents further orders. Otherwise the other powers’ orders resolve and draw-offering powers issue no orders; the missing-order defaults above apply. Draw offers reset each phase. Withdraw an offer before resolution to enter moves again.

Private scenarios

Choose Try a scenario to open an independent copy in a new tab. It starts from the current position, or the historical phase you are viewing. Click any country’s unit, then its destination: the sandbox switches power automatically, saves the move, and draws its arrow. Click the same unit twice to hold. For support or convoy, select the issuing unit, choose Support or Convoy, then click the cooperating unit and its destination. Click the supported unit twice to support a hold. Split coasts or multiple routes open a choice of complete orders. Press Escape on the map to cancel a selection. You control all seven powers and can resolve and continue exploring. Current copies include only your own ideas, never another player’s secret orders. Historical copies may include the publicly resolved orders. You can open several scenarios; none changes the live table.

Retreat planner

Only genuinely displaced units need retreat orders. A popup names each displaced army or fleet and its original province. Choose a legal destination from the dropdown, including an explicit coast where required, or choose Disband. Illegal destinations are not offered. If none of your units was displaced, the interface says so and does not ask you to retreat. Missing orders disband the displaced units when the phase resolves.

Winter build planner

When Winter adjustments begin, a panel beside the map shows your build allowance, chosen units, and remaining slots. On narrow screens the map stays visible above the scrolling choices. Choose Army or Fleet at each eligible home center, then Submit builds. Fleets are offered only at coastal centers. St Petersburg fleet builds require North coast (top) or South coast (bottom). Inland centers offer armies only. Occupied home centers and captured foreign centers are explained under “Why not the other controlled centers?” Units appear only when the host or clock resolves the phase. Unused builds are waived. Reopen the panel with Open builds & removals; powers with excess units use the same planner to choose removals.

Spain also has separate North and South coasts. Moving fleets see the full coast names in their order choices and the map’s territory details. Spain is a neutral center on the classic map, so capturing it increases your build allowance but never makes it a home build location.

Simple pieces and explicit convoys

Filled circles are armies; filled triangles are fleets. Territories use pastel country colors, with stronger unit fills and dark borders in the same hue. Double diamonds mark home supply centers; single diamonds mark other supply centers. A home center stays assigned to its original power even after capture. Only that original power can build there, while it owns the center and it is vacant. Both kinds count toward unit allowance and victory. The markers are not empty unit slots. An army’s Move via convoy action is separate from a direct move. If both are legal for a destination, the map asks whether to move directly or use a convoy. A fleet at sea uses Convoy an army: choose the army’s starting province, then its destination. The sheet spells out the army’s origin, destination, and the convoying fleet’s sea. The army must order the matching move via convoy, and every fleet along at least one complete sea route must enter a matching convoy order. Fleets in coastal provinces cannot convoy.

Order arrows and results

Movement arrows are solid white. Support and convoy lines are curved and dotted. Support-to-move arrows finish in the same direction as the move they support. After resolution, the resulting position keeps the previous phase’s orders visible: successful orders stay white; failed orders turn red with a cross marker. Each order uses its own result, so valid support can remain white even when the attack it supports bounces. Use Enter new orders or start a new order to return to planning, and Show last results to revisit the outcome. Historical boards show the starting position and the resolved orders, with the same failure colors.

Country outlines

Thick country outlines are generated from the same territorial control used for map colors. They expand around new holdings, including supply centers captured after Fall as Winter begins, and contract when territory is lost. Province lines inside one power’s territory remain thin. Original province shapes and legal movement borders do not change.

Reading territory colors

Supply-center ownership changes after Fall, so a Spring occupation does not immediately recolor an enemy center. Other land shows the last power to occupy it, with the original national colors until visited. Seas stay blue. Select any territory to read its full name, ownership, and units; use map zoom for narrow provinces.

Exports contain public state and resolved orders. The DBN export uses the published Diplomacy Broadcast Network schema; the native export retains full engine details and explicit convoy annotations.

09. Optional drunk country

The host can mark one unclaimed power as drunk during setup, before the first adjudication and before starting the clock. The other six countries are played normally. Each seated player can select the drunk country’s private order sheet and submit a separate movement proposal. A player has one ticket regardless of how many orders they enter.

At adjudication the server chooses uniformly among nonempty, submitted proposal sheets and uses that player’s entire sheet. Saved drafts and empty sheets are skipped. This is equivalent to rerolling the player die until a submitted sheet is found. If none exists, the country holds. Missing unit orders within a selected sheet also default to hold. Only the chosen sheet and player number are revealed in the phase history; other proposals stay private. Each phase starts with fresh proposal sheets.

Retreats and winter adjustments are automatic. Retreat destinations are randomized, avoiding collisions among that country’s own retreats whenever possible; enemy retreat collisions still follow normal adjudication. Units disband only if no compatible legal retreat is available. Builds randomly fill the available build allowance at legal, vacant, owned home centers, choosing armies or fleets and explicit coasts where legal. Removals are random and occur only when the supply-center count requires them.

Supply centers, territorial capture, combat, and victory rules are unchanged. Human powers can capture the drunk country’s centers. A unanimous draw is decided by the surviving human powers; the drunk power does not block it.
